Who breaks out next?

Ranking Europe’s under-23 players on how far they out-produce their positional peers, per 90 minutes, across the Big-5 leagues in 2025-26.

chartData = FileAttachment("breakout_chart.json").json()
players = chartData.players
top12 = players.filter(d => d.u23)
  .sort((a, b) => b.breakout_score - a.breakout_score)
  .slice(0, 12)
top12Names = new Set(top12.map(d => d.player))
u23 = players.filter(d => d.u23)

// deterministic horizontal jitter so same-age players don't stack into columns
jitter = (name) => {
  let h = 0;
  for (const c of name) h = (h * 31 + c.charCodeAt(0)) % 997;
  return (h / 997 - 0.5) * 0.72;
}
stage = crTriggerIndex ?? 0

Every dot is one of the 1,475 outfield players with at least 900 league minutes in Europe’s five biggest leagues in 2025-26. Height is the breakout score: seven per-90 metrics (non-penalty xG, expected assists, shots, key passes, xG chain, xG buildup, and real goals plus assists), each compared against players in the same position group and weighted by league strength. Zero means typical for your position.

Everyone left of the divider is 23 or younger: 483 players. The question is who is young and already above the line.

The twelve highest scorers under 23: Olise, Nuno Mendes, Yamal, Cherki, Bischof, O’Reilly, Fermín López, Karl, Doku, Garnacho, Güler, Fati. Familiar stars validate the method. The unfamiliar names are the point.

The dot to remember: Lennart Karl, 17, producing 2.2 standard deviations above the average Big-5 midfielder in his first real season. Only seven under-23 players in Europe out-produced him, all older, most already famous.

Production is only half the question

stage2 = Math.max(0, (crTriggerIndex ?? 0) - 4)
gems = new Set(["Lennart Karl", "Tom Bischof", "Lamine Yamal", "Arda Güler", "Alejandro Garnacho"])

Production says who is good now. Scouts also ask how much better a player can get. Across: the same breakout score. Up: remaining ceiling, FC 26’s potential minus current overall rating. Dot size is market value. Futures live up and to the right.

The two axes disagree on purpose. Finished breakout stars like Olise sit far right but low, because scouts already rate them in the 90s. Big-ceiling teenagers mostly sit left, not producing yet. The rare dots with both: Yamal, Bischof, Güler, Garnacho, and above everyone, Lennart Karl.

Read the vertical axis skeptically. “Potential” is a scouting opinion baked into a video game, it shrinks as players get famous, and 28 of 483 players had no confident match and are omitted. A second opinion, not ground truth.

The full shortlist

The ranked table behind both charts is in shortlist.csv, with every raw metric included so you can see why each player ranks where he does. Produced by a reproducible pipeline: make ingest scrape scrape-sofifa analyze render.

Honest caveats

One season. A small window; a hot half-season inflates per-90 rates.

Blunt league adjustment. Metrics are discounted by UEFA association coefficients (England 1.00 down to France 0.81). That corrects between leagues, not between schedules, and possession-heavy teams (PSG, Bayern, Barcelona) still inflate involvement metrics like xG chain.

Position bias. Scores compare players within FW/MF/DF groups, but the metric set leans attacking. Attacking full-backs (Nuno Mendes, O’Reilly) get flattered against a defender baseline.

xG-heavy by necessity. FBref stopped publishing advanced metrics on its public pages in 2026, so five of seven metrics come from Understat’s xG model. Treat second decimals as noise.

“Potential” is an opinion. The ceiling axis is a video-game scouting rating: subjective, fame-compressed, missing for 28 of 483 players.

Production is not destiny. This measures who is over-producing young. It knows nothing about injuries, temperament, or context beyond the numbers.
